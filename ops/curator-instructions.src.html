MalAvi · for curators

A guide to
curating MalAvi

When data are submitted through the standard template, you will get an email with a pdf report. You read the report and evaluate the submission and then you click a link in the pdf that takes you to a Google form (autofilled with the submission info) where you record your decision ("Accept", "Flag", or "Reject"; the latter two require written explanations).

If someone deposits a PDF in the system without a data template filled out, you will still receive an email but no PDF report. The email will prompt you to extract the data yourself, fill in a submission template, and submit it yourself. I have a program that can try to extract data from the paper, but it doesn't work for most papers because host-parasite associations are often not reported as tables, or there is extra info that is hard to capture for some other reason.

The emailed report

Live

When someone submits the data template file, a report is generated and it comes to you as an email from “malaviadmin@gmail.com”. The report goes through several dozen automatic checks of the novel sequences, the proposed names of new lineages, and the host and geographic data. But these are just some basic checks, you still need to review.

In case someone tries to reserve a lineage name that is wrong for some reason (e.g., already taken), the report will propose the next available name. If you accept the submission, you are also approving the suggested name (unless you flag it with a comment).

When you click the button in the report to make a decision, it will link to a Google forms doc that has all the submission info pre-filled out, and you will just have to answer a few questions.

Add malaviadmin@gmail.com to your contacts because that's the account sending the emails. The email is an ordinary Google account and it seems to be delayed getting to my university address, but it does work.

Recording your decision

Live

Click the link in the report and it takes you to a short form to fill out. Should be easy.

The three options you will be choosing:

Accept

This can go into the next release, as-is.

Waits 24 hours before anything happens, so another curator can also evaluate, if necessary.

Flag for further review

Choose this option if some part of the submission needs correcting or you are unsure about something and want another curator to review as well.

Blocks the submission until the curators can resolve it.

Reject

This should not be in MalAvi at all (any number of reasons could lead to this outcome; but we can contact the author if they submitted the data themselves for clarification).

Blocks the submission the same way a flag does. See below. The lead curator can still overrule the decision after discussion with the other curators.

A rejection blocks a submission, but does not finish it. When the curators have come to a final decision, the lead curator chooses “Close a submission for good” on the same verdict form and records a reason. That step ends the submission, meaning that the reserved lineage name(s) become available again, the submission leaves the public queue, and the submitter is informed 24 hours later; the delay is in place to give curators time to catch and fix a mistake in case one was made.

A submission somebody has already accepted cannot be closed. Flag it first, so the objection is on the record with your name against it.

When something is wrong with a submission

Live

If you want to make a correction, you must also flag the submission. In other words, a correction can't be made AND have the submission accepted at the same time.

Here's the breakdown of what a correction would look like:

  1. Flag the submission, and explain what's wrong and how it should be fixed.
  2. The lead curator has to approve after discussing with you and potentially the author who submitted the data. No correction is applied without that approval, and a lead cannot approve their own.
  3. The maintainer runs a program that applies the agreed upon changes.
  4. The corrected submission gets sent out with a new report (and a revision number) to you and the other curators for approval.

If a submission is unpublished work, it stays private. The public queue shows a claimed lineage name and the date it was claimed, and nothing else. A submission of unpublished work can be approved and hold its lineage name reservations indefinitely (i.e., until the authors publish their work and provide the additional data and publication).

The host and geography records are a separate question, and the submitter answers it. The form asks whether we may add their records now. If they say yes, the records go into the next release credited as, for example, “Ellis et al unpubl”, and are renamed to the real citation once the study is out. If they say hold, or did not answer, the records wait. Either way their lineage names are confirmed by a curator and the reservation stands, so the choice only decides when the records become public, never whether the submission is reviewed.
